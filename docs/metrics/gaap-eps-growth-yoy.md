### GAAP EPS Growth YoY

GAAP EPS Growth YoY is best calculated from **GAAP diluted earnings per share in the latest reported quarter** compared with the same quarter one year earlier.

GAAP means Generally Accepted Accounting Principles. It reflects reported earnings after operating costs, interest, taxes, and other accounting gains or losses. Diluted EPS also accounts for potential dilution from securities such as stock options. Comparing the same quarter across years helps control for seasonality, while the latest-quarter view makes recent changes easier to see than a TTM average.

Formula:

```text
GAAP EPS Growth YoY = (Latest Quarter GAAP Diluted EPS - Same Quarter Last Year GAAP Diluted EPS) / Same Quarter Last Year GAAP Diluted EPS
```

Or, expressed as a percentage:

```text
GAAP EPS Growth YoY % = ((Latest Quarter GAAP Diluted EPS - Same Quarter Last Year GAAP Diluted EPS) / Same Quarter Last Year GAAP Diluted EPS) * 100
```

For example, diluted EPS rising from $1.00 to $1.25 means GAAP EPS growth is +25%: the company reported 25% more earnings per share than in the comparable quarter one year ago.

The app calculates this percentage only when both quarters have positive EPS. A zero or negative comparison, a move from losses to profits, or a latest-quarter loss is treated as unavailable because an ordinary growth percentage would be misleading. Read the metric notes for the reason.

Why I’d put this next: revenue growth shows whether sales are expanding. GAAP EPS growth adds whether that expansion is translating into reported earnings per share before checking earnings quality with free cash flow.

### Tags

GAAP EPS Growth YoY is a business-growth metric, so its tags should describe reported earnings growth.

Suggested tags:

- **Strong:** Latest-quarter GAAP EPS growth YoY is 20% or higher. Reported earnings per share are growing quickly.
- **Solid:** Growth is at least 8% but below 20%. Reported earnings per share are growing at a healthy rate.
- **Modest:** Growth is at least 0% but below 8%. Reported earnings per share are flat or growing modestly.
- **Weak:** Growth is below 0%, with both quarters still profitable. Reported earnings per share are lower than a year ago.
- **Unclear:** The metric has no usable value and no explicit unavailable status.
- **Unavailable:** Comparable quarterly GAAP diluted EPS is missing from the current open-data source, or either quarter has zero or negative EPS.

These thresholds should be treated as a first-pass signal. Unusual gains, tax benefits, impairment charges, acquisitions, share buybacks, dilution, and a small prior-year earnings base can move EPS growth without a similar change in customer demand. Read the tag together with revenue growth, margins, free cash flow, share count, and sector.
