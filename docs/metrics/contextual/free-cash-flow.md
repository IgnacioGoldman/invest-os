# Free Cash Flow Margin

## What it means?

_How much of the company's sales remains as free cash flow?_

Measures the share of trailing twelve-month revenue left after operating cash flow pays for capital expenditure. Cash flow can vary with working capital, investment timing and the business model.

## How is calculated?

`(TTM operating cash flow − abs(TTM capex)) / TTM revenue × 100`.

All inputs must document twelve-month coverage, share the same period end and currency, and be finite; revenue must be positive. Normalize capex as a positive outflow; zero capex is usable only when explicitly reported. Never substitute quarterly FCF for TTM FCF. Keep recent-quarter negative cash flow, capex exceeding operating cash flow and quarterly YoY deterioration as explanatory notes, separate from the TTM measurement.

## How to interpret it?

A positive TTM margin can conceal negative recent cash flow. Review working capital, stock compensation and reinvestment needs. Financial businesses are not comparable.

This is a contextual metric. It has no deterministic classification rules or tags and does not participate in saved-filter matching, rankings or automatic scores.

## Display and history

Available in the expandable **Contextual metrics** section of stock details. The card opens its question, description, calculation, reporting dates, source identifiers and explanatory notes. Values and charts use neutral styling. Missing values remain unavailable, never zero.

The chart shows Quarterly Free Cash Flow Margin (separate from TTM). Plot only available comparable historical observations. Annual year-end valuation points and quarterly observations must keep their stated basis; neither is silently relabeled as current TTM data.

Source identifiers preserve provenance, not independent verification. See the [metric index](../README.md).
